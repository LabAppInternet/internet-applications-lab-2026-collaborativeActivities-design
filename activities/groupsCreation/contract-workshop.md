# Building your team: the contract workshop

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cooperative technique** | Team contract + cooperative base groups; brainwriting for the name |
| **Learning content** | Soft skills and methodology → Teamwork: *management of dependencies between members*, *task estimation* |
| **Week / level** | S1 (first day) |
| **Group setting** | Small group (30, 4 h) |
| **Duration** | 30 min |
| **Team size / roles** | 4 members, exceptionally 5; roles are assigned during the workshop |

You already know each other and you choose your own team. That is an advantage and a
risk: friends often skip the uncomfortable conversations — how much each person wants
to work, what happens if someone disappears — because they assume they agree. These
30 minutes make you have those conversations **now**, while nothing is at stake.

The output is the signed `TeamCommitmentContract`. Keep to the times: the name is the
most fun part, but it is not the most important one, so it is not first.

---

## Before you form a team: three questions

Ask these before committing to a team (in the break, before class):

1. **What grade are you aiming for?** Pass, Notable and Excellent need very different
   amounts of time.
2. **When can you work?** Mornings, evenings, weekends. Who has a job?
3. **How do you like to work?** Steadily every week, or in bursts before a deadline?

Teams have four members. A team of five needs the teaching staff's approval, and it
takes the extra *Quality & CI keeper* role.

---

## Activity 1 · Secret ballot on ambition — 5 min

Ambition mismatch is one of the most common sources of team conflict, and it is hard
to raise among friends.

1. Each member writes **privately** on a slip: target grade, hours per week they will
   really put in, and any fixed constraint (job, commute, weeks away).
2. Reveal at the same time.
3. Agree one target and one number of hours that everyone can sign honestly. A lower
   target that everyone meets is better than a high one that one person carries.

Reference: the course plans about 16–17 h per person per week outside class.

**Fills contract section 3.**

## Activity 2 · Roles — 3 min

Assign the first role to the person who is **not** already the strongest at it, and
fill the rotation table so that everyone holds at least two roles over the nine weeks.
Roles organise the team; every member still codes and owns at least one use case.

**Fills contract section 4.**

## Activity 3 · Norms tested against scenarios — 8 min

1. **(4 min)** Fill sections 5–8 with concrete values: hours, days, tools, names.
2. **(4 min)** Read these scenarios aloud and ask: **what exactly does our contract
   say we do?** If the answer is "nothing" or "it depends", amend it.
   - Week 6, deliverable due Friday. One member has not pushed anything for ten days
     and answers "yes, I'm on it".
   - Two members have disagreed on how to model an entity for three meetings.
   - `main` has been red since yesterday and nobody knows whose change broke it.

**Fills contract sections 5–8.**

## Activity 4 · Team name — 10 min

A good name is short, easy to say, has a reason behind it, and works as a repository
slug. You need one the whole team is comfortable saying in front of the class for
nine weeks, not a perfect one.

### Generate: brainwriting (4 min)

Do **not** discuss yet: the first person to speak usually wins.

1. Each member writes **two names** on a sheet in 1 minute.
2. Pass the sheet to the right and add **two more**, variations or combinations of
   what is already there. Repeat until the sheets have gone around the table.

**Where to look for ideas:**

- **The project** — a ticket-sales platform where many people try to buy the same seat
  at once: *seat*, *front row*, *sold out*, *encore*, *box office*, *backstage*,
  *race condition*, *lost update*, *double booking*.
- **The technology** — *201 Created*, *409 Conflict*, *418 I'm a teapot*, *merge*,
  *detached HEAD*, *Rollback*, *Null Pointers*.
- **You** — where you are from, a shared hobby, series or game, your usual meeting
  place.
- **Word games** — mix a project word with a personal one (*Sold-Out Squad*); build a
  word from your initials; translate into a language you share; use irony (*Works On
  My Machine*) or a metaphor (a band, a pit-stop crew, a heist crew).

### Filter and choose (4 min)

Cross out any name that could offend someone, is a real company or product, is longer
than three words, does not work as a lowercase-hyphenated slug, or cannot be explained
in one sentence. Then each member gives **3 dots** to the survivors; the top name wins
unless someone vetoes it with a reason (one veto each).

### Stuck? (2 min)

Write a **provisional name** and a date within the first week to confirm it. Do not
let the name eat the session. Write the one-sentence reason in section 1.

## Activity 5 · Read and sign — 4 min

The secretary reads the contract aloud; anyone may stop to ask for a change. Every
member signs and dates it, and the spokesperson hands it in.

---

## Checklist before signing

- [ ] Every blank has a concrete value.
- [ ] Grade target and hours come from the secret ballot, not assumed.
- [ ] Roles and rotation filled.
- [ ] The three scenarios have an answer in the contract.
- [ ] If there are five members, the teaching staff has approved it.

## If you want to go further (at home, optional)

- **"How I work" cards:** each member writes strengths, what they want to improve,
  how they work best and one thing that annoys them in team work; share them at the
  first weekly meeting.
- **Pre-mortem:** imagine the project has gone badly by week 9; list three concrete
  reasons and add a contract rule for each in the first review (section 11).

## Notes for the teaching staff

| Phase | Min | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Framing | 0 | Done in the one-line intro when handing out the contract. |
| Activity 1 | 5 | Secret ballot on ambition and hours. |
| Activity 2 | 3 | Roles and rotation. |
| Activity 3 | 8 | Norms, then three scenarios. |
| Activity 4 | 10 | Team name. |
| Activity 5 | 4 | Read aloud, sign, hand in. |
| **Total** | **30** | |

- Timings are estimates, not measured. The name is the most likely to overrun; the
  provisional-name rule is the release valve. Call time aloud at each phase.
- The roster has 75 students, which does not divide into teams of four: at least three
  teams of five are needed unless the numbers per small group change. Decide in
  advance how these are formed.
- While circulating, watch for teams that tick "Excellent" in seconds, leave sections
  5–8 blank, or where one person writes everything.
- Deliverable: one signed contract per team, read before the next session. It is
  evidence for the peer self-assessment at each checkpoint and for any mediation.
  Assessment is formative only.
